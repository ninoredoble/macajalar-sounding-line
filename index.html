    What is real here, and what is not

    Drawn to chart conventions

    Soundings are printed as numbers, with one decimal where the water is shallow. Depth tints step from dark in the shallows to pale in deep water. The border carries degrees and minutes, the scale bar is in nautical miles and kilometres, and the rose points to true north. The line, its distances, the interpolation and the fathom conversion (1 fathom is 1.8288 m) are computed live in your browser.

    Made up

    The coastline is a rough hand-drawn outline of the bay, with town positions that are only approximate. Depths come from a formula: water deepens with distance from the shore, plus one bank and one canyon placed by hand. The tide table is two sine waves. A real version would swap in a licensed bathymetric grid, such as GEBCO, and tide predictions from a hydrographic office.

    A lead line

    Before echo sounders, a sailor measured depth by lowering a weighted, marked rope until it touched bottom. The magenta dot on the chart and the vertical line in the profile play that part: drag along the profile to lower the lead anywhere on the line.
